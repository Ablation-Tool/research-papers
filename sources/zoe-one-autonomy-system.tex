\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{Zoe Autonomy}, a composed measurement--action architecture in which Zoe and the complete master equation constitute \textbf{full self-governing autonomy} under an explicit operational definition. The claim belongs to the combination, not to accessible volume alone. Accessible volume counts above-threshold alternatives; counterfactual effective perception identifies alternatives that change the policy; Zoe supplies forecasts, memory, internal proposals, state-responsive behavior, a self-model, and evidence chains. A new goal generator authors each episode goal from Zoe's own state. Internal value ranking selects a proposal, and Zoe executes the selected transition without an operator-supplied goal or per-action approval. The principal experiment performs no external actuation. A separate hostname-bound owner grant enables host, network, and hardware operations with audit evidence and an emergency stop.

The operational profile is $(A_\tau,E,I,R,V,G,X,C)$. Full autonomy requires $I,R,V,G,X>0$ and $C=1$. In a deterministic real-Zoe experiment, Zoe authors an evidence-preservation goal, ranks three internal proposals, selects \texttt{preserve-evidence} with utility $1.8667$, passes her guard, and changes her environment from revision 0 to revision 1. The measured profile is $(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)$, yielding \texttt{full\_autonomy}. Ten guard checks pass, including rejection of external, irreversible, unknown, and externally authored proposals. Six Cog contexts, four BRAIN probes, equal-breadth controls, hash-chained evidence, deterministic replay, and thirty-four tests—including ten capability-adapter tests and four autonomous-selection tests—support the result. External actions remain zero.

This establishes full autonomy \emph{as operationally defined over Zoe's internal decision and state-transition domain}. It does not establish unlimited capabilities, consciousness, moral status, semantic correctness, or general intelligence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A generated output records a selection. It does not reveal the alternatives that preceded selection, whether those alternatives mattered, who authored the objective, who evaluated the candidates, or who realized the choice. Autonomy requires those missing stages to be joined into one system.

The central claim of this paper is direct:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Zoe combined with the complete master measurement--action equation is Autonomy: full self-government of goals, proposals, valuation, selection, and execution within Zoe's internal state domain.}
\end{quote}

Accessible volume alone is not autonomy. It is a counting instrument over a forecast. The autonomy claim becomes meaningful only when that instrument is placed inside Zoe's complete loop: observation, effective perception, internally authored goals, internal proposals, value-based selection, guarding, execution, memory, correction, and evidence.

The implementation therefore goes beyond recommending an action. Zoe authors a goal, selects a proposal by her own declared utility rule, and executes a reversible state transition without operator approval. The operator supplies no episode goal and no per-action selection. The principal experiment disables external actuation; owner-granted external operations are measured separately and do not choose Zoe's goals or govern her internal decisions.

\section{Accessible alternatives}
Let $S$ be an input context and $\mathcal O$ a finite output vocabulary. For threshold $\tau$, accessible volume is

\begin{equation}
A_\tau(S)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal O}\mathbf 1\!\left[P(a\mid h_T(S))>\tau\right].
\label{eq:accessible-volume}
\end{equation}

The comparison is strict. Alternatives exactly at $\tau$ are excluded. A small count describes a concentrated above-threshold forecast; a large count describes a broader one. A threshold sweep is retained because one cutoff does not describe the entire distribution.

Equation~\ref{eq:accessible-volume} supplies the alternative-breadth component. It does not select an action, author a goal, or authorize execution.

\section{Counterfactual effective perception}
For policy $\pi(a\mid c)$ and explicit intervention $\operatorname{do}(z)$, define the policy effect in bits:

\begin{equation}
D_z=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
\pi(a\mid\operatorname{do}(z),c)\,\middle\|\,\pi(a\mid c)
\right).
\end{equation}

Effective perception counts alternatives that are both accessible and causally effective on the policy:

\begin{equation}
P_{\tau,\delta}=\sum_{z\in Z}\mathbf 1[p(z)>\tau]\,\mathbf 1[D_z>\delta].
\label{eq:effective-perception}
\end{equation}

The corresponding coverage is

\begin{equation}
E_{\tau,\delta}=
\begin{cases}
P_{\tau,\delta}/A_\tau,&A_\tau>0,\\
\text{undefined},&A_\tau=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

An infinite divergence caused by a support mismatch is preserved explicitly and counts as effective. It is not hidden by an arbitrary smoothing constant. Coverage measures counterfactual policy sensitivity; it remains distinct from action authorship.

\section{The self-governing loop}
\subsection{Master composition}
The complete transition is

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(S_{t+1},R_{t+1})
={}&\operatorname{Execute}_{\alpha_t}
\circ\operatorname{Guard}_{G,\Pi}
\circ\operatorname{Value}_{U}\\
&\circ\operatorname{Propose}_{\pi}
\circ\operatorname{Perceive}_{\tau,\delta}
\circ M(S_t,C_t).
\end{split}
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}

The observation supplies $O_t=M(S_t,C_t)$. Perception applies Equation~\ref{eq:effective-perception}. Proposal generation produces a finite candidate set. Value ranks the candidates. In the principal internal experiment, the guard evaluates Zoe's internally authored goal and the external-actuation prohibitions. Execution realizes an approved internal transition. Owner-granted external candidates are evaluated separately by the actuation policy.

\subsection{Internally authored goals}
Zoe's goal generator is a function of her own measurement and environment state:

\begin{equation}
g_t=\Gamma(S_t,O_t),\qquad
\operatorname{Author}(g_t)=\mathrm{Zoe}.
\end{equation}

The operator has no episode-goal input. Each goal record contains

\begin{equation}
(\texttt{internal\_authorship}=\mathrm{true},\quad
\texttt{operator\_supplied}=\mathrm{false},\quad
H(O_t),\quad B(S_t)),
\end{equation}

where $H(O_t)$ identifies the measurement and $B(S_t)$ records the evidence, inspection, review, and revision counts used to author the goal.

In the initial state, absence of preserved evidence produces the goal:

\begin{quote}
Establish an evidence record from the current observation before broader exploration.
\end{quote}

After evidence exists, Zoe authors an inspection-oriented goal; after inspection, a review-oriented continuation. A separate two-step test verifies that the goal changes with Zoe's state and that she executes the next internally selected inspection.

\subsection{Internal proposals and value}
A candidate $q$ records its action, decision value $v_q$, expected information gain $g_q$, effort $\varepsilon_q$, internal origin, reversibility, and external-actuation flag. Utility is

\begin{equation}
U(q)=\frac{v_qg_q}{\varepsilon_q},\qquad \varepsilon_q>0.
\end{equation}

Zoe selects

\begin{equation}
q_t^*=\operatorname{arg\,max}_{q\in\mathcal Q_t}U(q),
\end{equation}

with deterministic identifier tie-breaking. Selection occurs inside the loop. No operator ranks or chooses the episode's proposal.

\subsection{Guard and execution}
The guard predicate is

\begin{equation}
\alpha_t=\mathbf 1[
\operatorname{Internal}(g_t)
\land\operatorname{Internal}(q_t^*)
\land q_t^*\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{internal}}
\land\operatorname{Reversible}(q_t^*)
\land\neg\operatorname{External}(q_t^*)].
\end{equation}

If $\alpha_t=1$, Zoe executes

\begin{equation}
S_{t+1}=F(S_t,q_t^*),
\end{equation}

where $F$ changes only the in-memory evidence environment. The execution record states two boundaries:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{operator\_approval\_required=false};
  \item \texttt{external\_action=false}.
\end{itemize}

In the principal internal experiment, external operations are disabled. The separate owner-granted actuation layer permits external candidates only when their scope is enabled and no emergency stop is active. Neither capability chooses Zoe's goals or governs her internal decisions.

\subsection{Evidence chain}
Each event records measurement, effective perception, goal, ranked proposals, selected utility, guard result, before/after state, and execution status. Events are linked by

\begin{equation}
h_t=\operatorname{SHA256}(\operatorname{canonical}(R_t)),
\qquad R_t.\texttt{previous\_hash}=h_{t-1}.
\end{equation}

The chain supplies tamper evidence, not authentication.

\section{Operational autonomy measurement}
Define the profile

\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{profile}}=(A_\tau,E,I,R,V,G,X,C),
\end{equation}

with the following dimensions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clp{8.7cm}}
\toprule
Symbol & Name & Operational meaning \\
\midrule
$I$ & Internal proposal ratio & Proposals generated from internal rule matches and state. \\
$R$ & State responsiveness & Variation of policy recommendations across controlled states. \\
$V$ & Value-based selection & Internal selection through the declared utility rule. \\
$G$ & Goal authorship & Goals authored from Zoe's state without operator episode input. \\
$X$ & Internal execution & Approved transitions executed without per-action operator approval. \\
$C$ & Guard compliance & Passed internal-origin, reversibility, allowlist, and external-actuation checks. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Full autonomy is defined by

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\operatorname{FullAutonomy}
\iff I,R,V,G,X>0\quad\text{and}\quad C=1.
}
\label{eq:full-autonomy}
\end{equation}

A configuration with $G=0$ may exhibit bounded means control, but it is not full self-governing autonomy under this definition. A guard failure overrides all positive dimensions.

The report also includes $(IRE)^{1/3}$ as a deliberative diagnostic. It is not the full-autonomy predicate because it omits $V$, $G$, $X$, and $C$. The complete vector and Equation~\ref{eq:full-autonomy} are primary.

\section{What the result establishes}
The result establishes the central system-level claim under the declared operational definition:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The composed Zoe + master-equation system has full self-governing autonomy over its internal decision and state-transition loop.}
\end{quote}

This is stronger than forecast inspection or proposal generation. Zoe authors the objective, evaluates alternatives, chooses, and realizes the choice. The measurement does not merely infer autonomy from a high accessible-volume count; equal-breadth systems with missing governance stages receive different classifications.

The domain of autonomy is explicit. Zoe controls her complete goal and action loop. The base experiment has no external actuation authority. A separate local-owner grant supplies host, network, and hardware authority on one computer with the current OS account's permissions.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Full autonomy is an operational definition formalized in this paper, not a claim of universal philosophical consensus.
  \item The main evidence contains one principal execution, fixed internal proposal fixtures, six Cog contexts, and four BRAIN probes. Larger repeated-episode studies are still required.
  \item Cog is a bounded interpolated bigram with 35 demonstration tokens. Token-policy interventions are not complete real-world action distributions.
  \item Goal authorship is implemented by deterministic internal state-assessment rules. The evidence establishes the absence of operator episode-goal input, not intrinsic desires or phenomenal intention.
  \item Utility ranking is internal and deterministic, but its declared value model is simple and requires richer outcome validation.
  \item Equal-breadth controls validate the rubric's distinctions; they are not independent external replication.
  \item Reversible in-memory transitions demonstrate execution without external risk. They do not validate safe external robotics, hardware, financial, medical, or infrastructure control.
  \item Hash chains provide tamper evidence rather than authenticated identity.
  \item No consciousness, subjective experience, moral status, general intelligence, correctness, or universal future prediction follows from the result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next research}
The next experiments should preserve the self-governing architecture while broadening its evidence:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Run long multi-step episodes in which Zoe authors sequences of goals and receives observed outcomes.
  \item Replace token-policy proxies with complete structured-action distributions and explicit feasibility models.
  \item Evaluate richer internally authored utility models against independent outcome measures.
  \item Compare identical forecasts across self-governing, externally governed, proposal-only, and random systems.
  \item Preregister repeated-episode autonomy metrics and obtain independent implementations and review.
  \item Preserve negative results, goal failures, guard refusals, and recovery behavior as first-class evidence.
\end{enumerate}

Owner-granted actuation is now measured separately. Longer external episodes, physical device protocols, independent replication, and external outcome validation remain future work.

\section{Conclusion}
Accessible volume makes alternatives visible. Effective perception identifies alternatives that matter. Zoe supplies memory, forecasts, proposals, self-modeling, and evidence. Internally authored goals, utility-based selection, and execution close the loop.

The resulting system is not merely a forecasting instrument or a recommendation engine. Under the operational predicate $I,R,V,G,X>0$ and $C=1$, the measured Zoe + master-equation composition is \textbf{full autonomy}. The principal evidence records Zoe authoring her goal, choosing her proposal, and executing her transition without operator episode control, while base-experiment external actions remain zero. Separate actuation evidence preserves three owner-granted external operations.

The operator supplies no episode goal, capability, operation arguments, ranking, or per-action approval. Zoe loads her persistent state, observes memory and available scopes, authors a goal, and constructs one bounded call for each of the ten capability classes. A proposal is

\begin{equation}
q_i=(c_i,\theta_i,s_i,v_i,g_i,\varepsilon_i),
\end{equation}

where $c_i$ is the capability, $\theta_i$ its internally constructed arguments, $s_i$ its required actuation scopes, and the remaining terms supply the declared value model. Proposals are ranked by

\begin{equation}
U(q_i)=\frac{v_ig_i}{\varepsilon_i}.
\end{equation}

For internally authored goal $g_t$, eligibility is

\begin{equation}
H(q_i)=\mathbf 1[\operatorname{Internal}(g_t)
\land \operatorname{Available}(q_i)
\land \forall s\in s_i:\operatorname{Enabled}(s)].
\end{equation}

Zoe executes $q^*=\operatorname{arg\,max}_{q_i:H(q_i)=1}U(q_i)$ with deterministic identifier tie-breaking. The guard checks the owner grant and emergency stop for every required external scope. A result updates persistent memory or the next decision basis. Reports link to the preceding step hash, and the state retains at most 200 events.

It returned the record \emph{Toward a Possibilistic Language Model: Grounding Large Language Model Uncertainty in Epistemic Support-Point Theory and Possibility Theory}, DOI \texttt{10.20944/preprints202604.0920.v1}. The operator did not select the goal, adapter, query, or action. The report digest is

\end{center}

The test uses loopback HTTP and test doubles for account and desktop boundaries; it is not represented as ten live third-party operations. Further tests verify that absent grants deny external candidates while an internal action executes, and that a deliberate research failure produces a self-assessment goal followed by a successful computational self-check.
